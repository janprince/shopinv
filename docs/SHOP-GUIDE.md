# Using the shop manager

A short guide for the people behind the counter. No technical knowledge needed.

---

## Signing in

Go to the shop's web address and enter your username and password. Tap **Show**
if you want to check what you typed.

Forgotten your password? Ask the owner. They can set a new one for you — nobody,
including the owner, can see your old one.

If you see "This account has been switched off", ask the owner to switch it
back on.

---

## Recording a sale

This is the screen you will use most. Open **New sale**.

1. **Find the product.** Start typing its name or code in the search box, or
   scan its barcode. The box is already selected when the page opens, so you can
   just start typing.
   - On a laptop, press **Enter** to add the first product in the list.
   - Press **/** at any time to jump back to the search box.
2. **Add it.** Tap the product. It appears in *This sale* on the right.
3. **Change the amount** with the **−** and **+** buttons, or type the number.
4. **Remove** anything added by mistake.
5. **Choose how the customer is paying** — Cash, Mobile Money or Bank Transfer.
6. **For cash**, type what they handed you, or tap one of the suggested amounts.
   The change to give back appears in green.
7. **Tap Complete sale.** You will be asked to confirm the total. Check it, then
   confirm.
8. **Print the receipt** if the customer wants one, then **Start the next sale**.

### If something is wrong

- **"Only 3 left. Reduce the quantity to continue."** — someone has already sold
  some of that stock, or the shelf count is wrong. Lower the number, or check the
  shelf and record a correction afterwards.
- **The screen says you have no internet.** Finish serving the customer, but
  know that the sale is *not* recorded until the connection comes back. Your
  basket is safe on the device — nothing is lost. When the connection returns,
  tap Complete sale again. It cannot charge twice.
- **You pressed the button twice.** Nothing bad happens. The app recognises a
  repeat and shows you the sale that was already recorded.

### If a sale was completely wrong

Sales cannot be edited or deleted — that is deliberate, so the shop's records
can always be trusted. Ask the **owner** to reverse the sale. They will be asked
to write down why, the items go back into stock, and both the original sale and
the reversal stay on the record.

For a single item coming back, use **Stock adjustments → Customer return**
instead.

## Adding a product

**Products → Add product.**

Fill in the name, a short product code (like `SHEA-250`), the category, and how
it is sold — pieces, bottles, kilograms and so on. This matters: something sold
by the **piece** cannot be sold as half a piece, but something sold by the
**kilogram** can.

Then the cost price (what you pay) and the selling price (what the customer
pays), and the level at which you want to be warned to reorder.

If you already have some on the shelf, type it under **Opening stock**. If not,
leave it blank and record it later through Receive stock.

## When a delivery arrives

**Receive stock.**

Choose the product, type how much arrived and what you paid per unit. Add the
supplier, the batch number from the packaging, and the expiry date if it has
one — these are optional but they make life much easier later.

You will then see a **review screen**. Nothing has been saved yet. Check the
numbers, especially the quantity, then tap **Confirm**.

## When stock is lost, damaged or comes back

**Stock adjustments.** Choose the product, then what happened:

| Choose | When |
| --- | --- |
| **Damaged stock** | Broken, spoiled or unsellable. |
| **Expired stock** | Past its date, taken off the shelf. |
| **Missing stock** | Cannot be found during a count. |
| **Customer return** | Brought back and can be sold again. |
| **Positive correction** | A count found *more* than the app expected. |
| **Negative correction** | A count found *less* than the app expected. |

Always write a real reason. In three months, that sentence is the only thing
that explains why the number changed. "Bottle broke when the crate was dropped"
is useful; "correction" is not.

You will see the current amount and what it will become before anything is
saved. Large changes ask you to confirm twice.

## Keeping an eye on things

The **Dashboard** answers the everyday questions: what has been sold today, what
needs restocking, what is about to expire, what has just happened.

Anything on the dashboard can be tapped to see the detail behind it.

**Expiry watch** lists everything past its date or close to it, soonest first,
with a Write off button beside each one.

**Stock history** shows every single change to every product — what changed, by
how much, who did it, and why. Nothing can be removed from it.

## For the owner

You see everything a shopkeeper sees, plus:

- **Cost prices and profit estimates** throughout the app.
- **Reports** — sales over time, by product, by category, by payment method,
  inventory value, expiry, adjustments and estimated profit. Every one can be
  downloaded as a spreadsheet.
- **Reversing a sale**, with a reason that stays on the record.
- **Users** — add staff, switch accounts off, set new passwords.
- **Shop settings** — the shop's name and address for receipts, and how early to
  be warned about low stock and expiry dates.
- **Audit history** — every sign-in, price change, stock change, sale and
  reversal. It cannot be edited or deleted by anyone, including you.

### About the profit figure

Where the app shows profit, it means **money in, minus what those goods cost the
shop**. It does **not** subtract rent, wages, transport, electricity or tax, so
it is not the profit your accountant will calculate. It is a fast, honest guide
to whether the shop is selling at a healthy margin — nothing more.

---

## Habits worth keeping

- Record sales as they happen, not at the end of the day.
- Record breakages the moment they happen, while you remember why.
- Count one shelf a week and post a correction if it disagrees with the app.
- Check **Expiry watch** every Monday.
- Everyone uses their own account — that is what makes the history mean anything.
